Direct answer

A bust-size reference chart relates your underbust to the difference between bust and underbust measurements. Subtract the snug underbust from the standing bust using the same unit. This gives a starting cup index in the site's simplified sequence, not the physical volume of a finished bra cup. Use the result as a starting point and check the specific garment's fit.

Worked example

With a 32-inch snug underbust and a 36-inch standing bust, the difference is 36 − 32 = 4 inches. In the site's basic US/Canada sequence, that difference maps to D, producing a 32D starting label. It does not guarantee that every 32D bra will fit the same way.

What the Bust Measurement Actually Means

The bust figure in a chart is an approximate body measurement around the fullest part of the chest. It is not the same thing as the physical volume of a bra cup, and it should not be used to predict exactly how a particular bra will look or fit.

For example, two people can both measure 38 inches around the bust while having different underbust measurements. Their starting bra sizes can therefore be different. Breast shape and the construction of the bra add another layer of variation.

How to Read an Example

Suppose your snug underbust is around 32 inches and your standing bust is around 36 inches. The four-inch difference is the important part of this simple reference method. It corresponds to a D-cup starting point in the site's basic US/UK progression.

That does not mean every 32D bra will fit you, nor that every person measuring 36 inches at the bust should wear the same size. The chart translates measurements into a label; the garment determines the final fit.

Why Bust Circumference Alone Is Not Enough

A bust circumference without an underbust measurement tells you very little about the band-and-cup combination. The same bust circumference can occur on a narrower or wider ribcage, producing different cup relationships.

This is why Bra Size's main calculator asks for both snug underbust and standing bust measurements. Optional measurements can add context, but the basic result is intentionally presented as a starting point.
