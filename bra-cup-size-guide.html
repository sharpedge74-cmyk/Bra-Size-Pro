Direct answer

A cup letter is one part of a bra-size label, not a fixed measure of breast size. The cup position is read together with the band number and sizing system. US, UK, EU, and Australian labels can use different letter sequences, and manufacturers may differ too. Use a cup chart to understand labels, then check the specific bra's chart and fit.

What a Cup Letter Means

A bra size combines a band number and a cup letter. The band number identifies a size category around the ribcage; the cup letter marks a step in the cup sequence used by that sizing system. Neither part describes the full size on its own.

This is why a D cup is not one fixed volume that stays identical across all band sizes. A 30D and a 38D have different band numbers and different cup dimensions. To understand a label, read the number and letter together.

Lettering varies between brands and regions. Some US charts use DDD, some use F, and some show both. UK charts commonly use additional double-letter steps. The calculator's sequence is a consistent reference for its own results, not a promise that every shop uses the same labels.

Worked Example: How the Letter Is Chosen

Imagine a snug underbust measurement of 32 inches and a standing bust measurement of 36 inches. The difference is 4 inches. In the simplified US sequence shown above, a rounded difference of 4 maps to D, so the calculator displays 32D as its starting label.

The result comes from a defined calculation. It does not measure the finished cup of a particular bra, and it cannot account for every difference in garment shape, depth, fabric or construction. The full sequence and calculation steps are available in the Bra Sizing Methodology.

Why Cup Letters Change With Band Size

Imagine keeping the cup letter the same while changing the band number. The resulting size is not physically identical, because cup dimensions are graded alongside the rest of the garment. Sister sizing describes a related pattern: when the band moves one step smaller, the cup letter commonly moves one step larger; when the band moves larger, the cup letter commonly moves smaller.

For example, 34D and 32DD are commonly treated as sister sizes within a compatible sizing sequence. They are related labels, not identical bras. Read the full explanation in Sister Sizing Explained.

Why International Cup Labels Can Look Different

US, UK, EU, Australian and other sizing systems do not share one universal cup-letter sequence. The differences become more noticeable beyond D, where one system may use double letters while another advances to a different letter. Band labels also differ between systems, so an international conversion involves more than replacing one cup letter.

The Brand Size Converter compares selected labels, and the Bra Measurement Chart brings common size references together. A brand's own chart remains the definitive reference for the labels it uses.

What a Cup Chart Cannot Tell You

A cup chart explains a naming convention; it does not describe every detail of a bra's construction. Two garments with the same label may have different cup depth, width, wire shape, fabric stretch or strap placement. This is why a label alone cannot guarantee that two bras will feel the same.

When a calculated label and a garment do not seem to match, that difference does not automatically mean the measurement was recorded incorrectly. The calculation, the brand's size chart and the shape of the particular garment are separate pieces of information.
