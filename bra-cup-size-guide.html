Cup Letters Are Relative to Band Size

A cup letter does not describe an absolute breast volume. The same letter on different band sizes represents different cup dimensions. This is why sister sizes move diagonally through a sizing system rather than keeping the same cup letter.

What This Chart Can and Cannot Tell You

A measurement-based result is a useful starting point, not a guarantee of fit. Research comparing traditional measurement with professional fit criteria found meaningful differences between calculated and best-fit sizes, supporting the use of garment fit checks alongside measurements. (White & Scurr 2012, Ergonomics)

How to Read a Cup-Difference Chart

A measurement-difference chart starts with the distance between the fullest bust measurement and the underbust measurement. For example, if those measurements are about 34 inches and 30 inches, the difference is about 4 inches. Under the simplified convention used by BraSizePRO, that points to a D cup starting point.

This is best understood as a way to turn measurements into a starting label—not as a measurement of the breast itself. Real bodies are three-dimensional, and commercial bras are made in many different shapes.

Why Your Measured Size May Not Fit Perfectly

It is completely normal for the first size you calculate to need adjustment. A calculator cannot see whether a particular cup is too shallow, whether the wire is too narrow, or whether the band stretches more than expected. Research comparing measurement-based sizing with professional fit assessment has also found meaningful differences between calculated and best-fit sizes.

When you try a bra, look at the band, cups, wires and straps together. If the size is close but one area is wrong, a sister size or a different cup shape may be more useful than simply moving several cup letters at once.

When to Check the Brand's Own Chart

Always check the manufacturer's size chart when you are ready to buy. International labels are not perfectly interchangeable, and even brands using the same nominal size can construct their cups differently. Recent research has found measurable variation in cup volume between manufacturers, particularly as cup size increases.
