Why Measurement Methods Differ

Sizing systems use different measurement conventions. Rather than treating one historical method as universally correct or incorrect, use the measurement instructions and size chart provided for the specific brand or garment.

What You Need Before Starting

  • Soft measuring tape: Flexible sewing tape measured in inches or centimeters.
  • Mirror: To verify that the tape remains completely horizontal and level across your back.
  • Comfortable posture: Stand upright with relaxed shoulders. Do not suck in your stomach or puff out your chest.

Useful Measurements

Step 1: Loose Underbust

Place the tape directly beneath your breasts where the underwire sits (the inframammary fold). Keep the tape level across your back and pull it comfortably, as if wearing a gentle, relaxed garment.

Step 2: Snug Underbust

At the exact same position, pull the tape with moderate tension—the exact firmness you would want your bra band to hug you on its loosest clasp hook.

Step 3: Tight Underbust

Exhale completely and pull the tape as tight as you possibly can ("break the tape" tension). This identifies how much soft tissue compresses on your ribcage and prevents bands from shifting around during physical activity.

Step 4: Standing Bust

Stand tall and wrap the tape around the fullest part of your breasts, keeping the tape level. Ensure the tape touches the skin without denting or squishing breast tissue.

Optional: Leaning Bust (90 Degrees)

Bend forward at the waist so your torso is parallel to the floor. Let your breast tissue hang freely with gravity. Wrap the tape around the fullest part. This can provide additional information about breast shape and distribution, but it is not used as a universal correction factor by BraSizePRO.

Optional: Lying Bust

Lie flat on your back on a firm bed. Measure across the fullest part of your chest. This can provide another reference measurement, but it should not be treated as a universal indicator of cup size.

Make the Measurement Repeatable

The best home measurement is not the one that produces the smallest or largest number. It is the one you can repeat. Keep the tape in the same position, use the same posture, and avoid pulling harder simply because you think a particular size should come out.

If possible, take each measurement twice. Small differences are normal; a large difference usually means the tape moved, the tension changed, or your posture changed.

Common Measuring Mistakes

  • Tilting the tape: a tape that slopes up or down changes the circumference.
  • Pulling the tape too tightly: this can produce an artificially small underbust.
  • Using a padded bra: padding changes the outer measurement around the bust.
  • Holding your breath: this changes the ribcage measurement and makes the result harder to repeat.
  • Measuring over thick clothing: extra fabric becomes part of the circumference.

Which Bust Measurement Should You Use?

For the main BraSizePRO calculation, the standing bust measurement is the primary bust input. Leaning and other optional measurements can be useful references, especially when your breast tissue changes position significantly with posture, but they are not treated as universal correction formulas.

That distinction matters because there is no single body position that perfectly predicts how every bra will contain and support tissue.

Check Your Numbers Before Calculating

Before entering your measurements, ask yourself three simple questions: Was the tape level? Was I standing naturally? Did I measure at the same points each time? If the answer is yes, your numbers are ready for the Universal Bra Size Calculator.

What to Do With the Result

Think of the calculator's answer as your first fitting room number. Compare it with the brand's chart, try the garment on if possible, and pay attention to the band, cup coverage, wire position and overall comfort. A different size or shape can be a better match even when your measurements have not changed.
