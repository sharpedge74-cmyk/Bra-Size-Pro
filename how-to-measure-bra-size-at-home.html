Direct answer

To get a BraSizePRO starting size at home, measure your snug underbust directly below the bust and your standing bust around the fullest part. Keep the tape level and close to the body without painful pressure. Record both in the same unit, then enter them into the calculator. The result is a starting point, not a verdict.

Before you begin

Use a flexible measuring tape and record your numbers. A mirror can help you check the tape's position across your back. Keep the tape placement and tension as consistent as possible when repeating a measurement. ISO 8559-1 provides standardized definitions for clothing-related body measurements; it is not a bra-size formula.

The Two Main Measurements

1. Snug Underbust

Place the tape around the body directly below the bust, where the lower edge of a bra band normally sits. Keep it level all the way around and comfortably snug, without pulling so tightly that it digs into the skin. Record the measurement in inches or centimeters.

2. Standing Bust

Stand in a natural position and wrap the tape around the fullest part of the bust. Keep the tape level and close to the body without pressing inward. Record the measurement using the same unit as the underbust reading.

Keeping the Measurements Consistent

  • Keep the tape level: A tape that angles up or down can change the circumference reading.
  • Use steady tension: Pulling harder on one reading than another makes comparisons less reliable.
  • Keep the tape close: Thick clothing and padding can affect the number recorded.
  • Stand naturally: Use a relaxed posture and breathe normally.
  • Repeat the reading: If the second measurement differs noticeably, check the tape position and repeat it.

Optional Measurements in Other Guides

Some sizing methods also record a looser or tighter underbust, or measure the bust while leaning forward. These readings can describe a broader measurement range, but they are not inputs to the primary result from Bra Size's main calculator. The calculator uses snug underbust and standing bust.

Enter Your Measurements

Once recorded, enter the two main measurements in the Universal Bra Size Calculator. The calculator converts centimeter inputs internally when needed and uses the selected sizing system to display a starting label.

How the Numbers Become a Size

The main calculator uses snug underbust to determine a band-size starting point. It then compares standing bust with snug underbust and maps the rounded difference to a cup label in the selected system. The full process is documented in the Bra Sizing Methodology.

Different brands may use different size charts, and the same label can appear on garments with different shapes and construction. The result is therefore best understood as a consistent measurement reference—not a promise that every garment with that label will fit identically.

Common Sources of Variation

A small change in tape position, tension, posture, or unit conversion can change a recorded number. Differences can also come from the sizing system used by a particular brand. Keeping a record of the actual measurements makes it easier to understand why two calculators or charts may display different labels.

How we calculated this

Band: convert the snug underbust to inches, round to the nearest whole inch, then move to the next even value when the rounded number is odd.

Cup: subtract snug underbust from standing bust, round the difference to the nearest whole inch, then use that number as the index in the selected cup sequence. Negative differences are treated as zero.

Optional readings: tight underbust and leaning bust may be useful personal references, but they do not change the main calculator's result.

Measurement checklist

  • Place the tape directly below the bust for snug underbust.
  • Keep the tape level around the body.
  • Use steady tension without painful pressure.
  • Measure the standing bust around the fullest part without compressing the bust.
  • Record both measurements in the same unit.
  • Repeat a reading if the tape slipped, twisted, or was pulled tighter than before.

What can change the result?

Tape position, tape tension, posture, and unit conversion can affect the recorded numbers. The selected sizing system also changes the cup label used for a given index. Research comparing a traditional tape-measure method with professional fitting criteria found that the approaches could produce different recommendations in the study sample.
