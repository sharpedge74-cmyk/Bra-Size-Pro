Find a possible explanation for a fit issue

Choose the fit issue you notice and enter your current band and cup labels. The checker offers possible factors and adjustments to compare. It cannot identify the correct size from one symptom. Fit depends on the bra's shape, construction, and how it sits on your body.

How this fit guidance works

This tool is a symptom-based reference, not a medical diagnosis. It looks up the selected fit issue in Bra Size's guidance data, then compares nearby cup positions or band options where the issue supports that kind of fit experiment.

  • Input: your current band, cup, and the fit issue you want to investigate.
  • Guidance: the tool identifies a possible fit factor and a practical adjustment to compare.
  • Size comparison: some issues generate an adjacent cup or sister-size comparison; others are better addressed by checking cup shape, wire width, strap adjustment, or construction.

These suggestions are not universal rules. Fit depends on the garment, body shape, construction, and personal comfort. See the practical fit checklist.

Worked example: band rides up

If your current label is 36C and the back band rides up, compare a smaller band with the next cup position, such as 34D, if available. This is a fit experiment, not a guaranteed correction. Recheck cup coverage, wire position, and comfort in the actual bra.

Limits and comfort first

  • A fit sign can have more than one explanation. This tool offers possibilities, not a diagnosis or guaranteed size recommendation.
  • Check the band, cups, wires, center front, and straps together.
  • Do not keep wearing a bra that causes ongoing pain or skin injury.
  • For persistent pain, a new lump, or concerns related to pregnancy or surgery, speak with a qualified clinician.
