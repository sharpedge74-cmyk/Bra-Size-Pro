This guide covers 224 band-and-cup labels from 26AA through 52K. A calculator gives a measurement-based starting point; this reference explains how each label fits into the guide’s band and cup sequence.

A bra size has two parts. The number identifies the band category around the ribcage, while the cup letter identifies a cup step relative to that band. A cup letter is therefore not an absolute volume measurement; the band number changes the physical cup associated with the letter.

How to Use This Guide

Use your measurements to get a starting size, then use the entries below to understand that label. When you shop, check the manufacturer's own chart and assess the actual garment. Brand conversion charts are generally starting references because construction and fit can vary between brands and styles.

The individual entries use a simplified measurement-difference convention: AA is about a 0-inch difference, A about 1 inch, B about 2 inches, and each following step increases the reference difference by roughly one inch. This is useful for explaining the size labels, but it is not a universal manufacturing specification.

What Changes When You Move Between Sizes?

Keeping the band number and changing the cup letter changes the cup step. Keeping the cup letter and changing the band changes the physical cup as well as the band. This is the basic idea behind sister sizing: nearby sizes can have related cup capacity while using different band numbers and cup letters.

If the band feels comfortable and level but the cups are too small, look at a larger cup on the same band first. If the cups are close but the band is too loose or too firm, an adjacent sister size may be worth trying. A different cup shape can also solve a problem that changing size alone cannot.

Why the Same Label Can Fit Differently

A size label does not describe every detail of a bra's construction. Cup depth, cup width, wire shape, fabric stretch, strap placement and pattern grading all affect how the garment sits. This is why specialist size charts advise shoppers to use conversions as a starting point and then consider the actual fit.

Before You Buy

  1. Take a snug underbust and standing bust measurement with a flexible tape.
  2. Use the Bra Size calculator for a measurement-based starting point.
  3. Find your resulting band and cup in this guide to understand the label.
  4. Check the manufacturer's own chart, especially for extended bands and cups.
  5. When trying the bra, judge the band, cups, wires and overall comfort together.

Extended Sizes Need Extra Care

The presence of a size in this guide does not mean that every retailer or manufacturer offers it. Current specialist charts demonstrate that availability can begin at 28 and that UK-style cup progressions can extend through K, while another brand may use a different range. Treat 26, 50, 52 and extended cup sizes as particularly brand-dependent rather than assuming universal availability.

Bra Size uses size labels as reference points, not guarantees. If your exact size is difficult to find, do not automatically move several band sizes upward just to obtain a larger cup. Use sister sizing deliberately and check the fit of the actual garment.
