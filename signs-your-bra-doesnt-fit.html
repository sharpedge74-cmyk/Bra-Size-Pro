Direct answer

A bra may not fit well if its band shifts, cups cut in or gape, straps keep slipping, or wires press on breast tissue. These are clues to review the whole garment, not proof that one specific size is wrong. Band fit, cup shape, wire placement, strap adjustment and construction can all affect comfort. Measurements are a starting point, not a verdict.

1. The Back Band Rides Up

A proper bra band forms a continuous horizontal line parallel to the floor all the way around your ribcage. If the back curves up toward your neck or shoulder blades, your band is too large.

2. Spillage Over the Tops or Sides of the Cups (The "Quad-Boob")

Spillage can indicate insufficient cup coverage or a shape mismatch. Do not assume that moving up a fixed number of cup sizes is always the correct adjustment.

3. The Center Gore Floats Off Your Chest

The center bridge connecting the two underwires must lay completely flat against your sternum (breastbone). A floating gore can be associated with cup volume, cup shape, wire width, or garment construction. Check the overall cup and wire fit rather than assuming one size change will solve it.

4. Shoulder Straps Dig In Painfully

Strap pressure can have several causes, including strap adjustment, band fit, cup fit, and garment design. Check the band and cups together before changing size.

5. Straps Keep Slipping Off Your Shoulders

A loose or poorly adjusted band can contribute to slipping, but shoulder shape, strap length, cup fit, and garment design can also matter.

6. Cups Gap or Wrinkle at the Top

While gapping can mean cups are too big, it is often caused by a shape mismatch—such as shallow molded foam cups that fight against naturally projected or tear-drop breast tissue.

7. Underwires Poke Breast Tissue on the Sides

If wires dig into breast tissue near your armpit, the cup is too narrow. A wire that sits on breast tissue can indicate a width, cup-volume, shape, or construction mismatch. Compare the wire position with the breast crease and the specific garment design.

8. Underwires Slide Down Your Stomach

If underwires slide several inches below your breasts, the cups lack immediate projection at the bottom, forcing the bra downward.

9. You Rush to Take Your Bra Off the Second You Get Home

A Simple Fit Check You Can Do at Home

Put the bra on normally and check it from the front, side and back. Then move your arms, bend forward and take a few comfortable breaths. You are looking for a bra that stays reasonably stable while allowing normal movement.

  • Check that the band remains level around the torso.
  • Check that breast tissue is contained without persistent cutting-in.
  • Check that the wires, if present, follow the breast crease rather than sitting on tissue.
  • Check that straps provide adjustment without needing to be pulled extremely tight.

What to Change First

If the band is clearly too loose but the cup looks good, a sister size may be worth trying. If the band seems secure but the cups cut in, look at cup volume and shape. If the cups gape, do not automatically assume they are too large—a mismatch between the cup shape and your breast shape can create empty space too.

These are starting points, not rigid rules. The same visible symptom can have more than one cause.

Comfort Matters Too

A bra does not need to leave you completely unaware that you are wearing it, but persistent pain, pinching or strong pressure is a good reason to stop and reassess the fit. Research on bra fitting has found substantial differences between self-selected or measurement-derived sizes and professional fit assessments, which is one reason practical fit checks matter.

Worked example: reading more than one fit clue

Imagine a bra's back band rides up while the shoulder straps need repeated tightening. Those clues may point toward checking band tension and strap adjustment, but they do not prove that the band is the only problem. Recheck the cups, strap placement, and overall garment construction before deciding whether to try another size.

This is an illustrative fit-check scenario, not a diagnostic rule. The same symptom can have more than one cause.

A practical fit check

Check the bra from the front, side and back. Move your arms and bend forward comfortably. Notice whether the band stays reasonably level, the cups contain tissue without persistent cutting-in, and the wires, if present, sit around rather than on breast tissue. The details will vary with bra style.

What to try first

  • If the band rides up, check the band tension and whether the straps are over-tightened.
  • If cups cut in or spill over, compare cup volume and shape rather than automatically jumping several sizes.
  • If cups gape, check cup shape, strap adjustment and whether the style suits your shape.
  • If wires poke or press painfully, take the bra off and reassess the wire position, cup shape and construction.

These are possibilities, not one-to-one diagnoses. A study comparing traditional measurement sizing with professional fit criteria found that the methods could produce different recommendations in its sample. Read White & Scurr (2012).
