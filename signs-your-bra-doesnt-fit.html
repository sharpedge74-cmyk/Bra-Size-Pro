1. The Back Band Rides Up

A proper bra band forms a continuous horizontal line parallel to the floor all the way around your ribcage. If the back curves up toward your neck or shoulder blades, your band is too large.

2. Spillage Over the Tops or Sides of the Cups (The "Quad-Boob")

If your breasts bulge over the neckline or create four distinct lumps under a fitted t-shirt, your cup volume is at least 1 to 2 sizes too small.

3. The Center Gore Floats Off Your Chest

The center bridge connecting the two underwires must lay completely flat against your sternum (breastbone). If it tilts outward or perches on top of breast tissue, you need deeper or larger cups.

4. Shoulder Straps Dig In Painfully

Deep red indentations in your shoulder muscles indicate that the shoulder straps are carrying the entire weight of your bust. Tighten or size down the band to transfer the load back to your torso.

5. Straps Keep Slipping Off Your Shoulders

Loose bands cause the back strap anchors to drift outward toward your arm sockets, causing straps to slide down constantly.

6. Cups Gap or Wrinkle at the Top

While gapping can mean cups are too big, it is often caused by a shape mismatch—such as shallow molded foam cups that fight against naturally projected or tear-drop breast tissue.

7. Underwires Poke Breast Tissue on the Sides

If wires dig into breast tissue near your armpit, the cup is too narrow. A larger cup provides wider wires that trace the outer curve of your ribcage without cutting into breast roots.

8. Underwires Slide Down Your Stomach

If underwires slide several inches below your breasts, the cups lack immediate projection at the bottom, forcing the bra downward.
