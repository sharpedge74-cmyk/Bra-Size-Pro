Direct answer

This chart compares common bra-size labels across several regional systems. Use it to understand how band and cup labels may be written in another market, not as a guarantee that two labels represent identical garments. Conversion conventions vary by brand, especially for cup letters. When buying a specific bra, the manufacturer's current chart is the best reference.

These are common labeling conversions, not measurements mandated for every commercial bra. ISO 8559-1 provides standardized definitions for anthropometric measurements used in clothing sizing; it does not prescribe one universal bra-size conversion table.

How to Use This Chart Without Getting Lost

Start with your measurements rather than starting with a country label. Measure your underbust and bust using the method described in our at-home measuring guide, then use the chart to understand how a size may be labelled in another system.

The important word here is may. Conversion charts are useful for narrowing down a size, but they cannot account for every brand's grading, fabric stretch or cup shape.

Band Numbers Are Not Universal Measurements

A band number such as 32, 70 or 10 should not be read as though every system is simply printing the same body measurement in a different unit. Different markets developed different labeling conventions. That is why a numerical conversion can look surprising when you first compare systems.

For example, a commonly used conversion places a US/UK 32 near EU 70, AU 10 and FR/ES 85. The labels are related, but the garment's actual measurement and fit still depend on the manufacturer.

Why Cup Conversions Need More Care

Cup letters are particularly easy to misread because the progression is not identical between systems. Double letters and extended cup ranges are handled differently, and some manufacturers use their own labeling conventions.

So if you are converting an existing size for a particular bra, use the manufacturer's chart whenever one is available. Treat a general conversion table as a starting point, not a promise that two bras will fit identically.

Worked example: reading a band conversion

As a general reference example, US/UK band label 32 is commonly paired with EU 70 and AU 10. These are label equivalents, not three direct measurements of the body. Confirm the mapping on the target brand's current chart before relying on it for a purchase.

How to use this page

ISO 8559-1 defines clothing anthropometric measurement terms; it does not set a universal bra conversion chart. A 2025 study comparing bras from seven manufacturers found variation in cup volume across the products tested. Regional tables here are general references. If a brand’s current chart conflicts with a general table, follow the brand’s chart for that product. Read How to Measure at Home and the methodology.
