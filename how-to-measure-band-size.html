Direct answer

To measure your bra band starting point, place a flexible tape around your torso directly below the bust. Keep it level and snug without digging into the skin. Repeat the reading to check consistency. BraSizePRO uses the snug underbust in its formula, but the calculated band is a starting label—not a guarantee of comfort or fit.

How to Measure the Underbust

A bra band is one part of the overall fit. The goal is a secure, comfortable measurement that can be compared with the target garment chart:

  • Keep the tape level around the torso.
  • Measure snugly without digging the tape into the skin.

A band that rides up or feels uncomfortable is a reason to reassess the overall fit, including band tension, cup fit, strap adjustment, and garment construction.

Optional Tight Underbust Reference

  1. Loose Underbust: Tape is relaxed; skin is lightly touched.
  2. Snug Underbust: Moderate tension; your ideal band feeling.
  3. Tight Underbust: Exhale normally and pull the tape firmly enough to record a compressed reference measurement without hurting yourself.

A tighter measurement can provide additional context, but Bra Size does not use a fixed difference threshold to prescribe a smaller band. Compare your measurements with the specific manufacturer's chart and the actual garment fit.

Step-by-Step Underbust Measurement

  1. Place the tape directly underneath the breasts, where the lower edge of a bra band or underwire normally sits.
  2. Check the mirror to make sure the tape is level all the way around your torso.
  3. Pull it snugly enough to stay in place without deliberately compressing your body.
  4. Breathe normally and note the measurement in inches or centimeters.
  5. Repeat the measurement once or twice. If the readings differ noticeably, reposition the tape and try again.

A consistent measurement is more useful than trying to force the tape to a particular number.

Loose, Snug and Tight Measurements

You may see guides asking for loose, snug and tight underbust measurements. They describe different levels of tape tension and can be useful when assessing how much the ribcage and soft tissue change with tension.

  • Loose: the tape rests around the body with minimal tension.
  • Snug: the tape is firm and secure without intentionally compressing the body.
  • Tight: the tape is pulled firmly to show the body's compressed measurement.

These measurements provide context; they do not automatically mean that you should choose a particular band size. Bra Size uses the snug underbust as its main starting measurement and leaves garment-specific decisions to the actual fit.

What About the Old “+4” Rule?

The “+4” approach is one historical method of turning an underbust measurement into a band label. It should not be treated as a universal rule for every modern bra or every sizing system. A measurement-based starting point and the manufacturer's own chart are more useful when you are choosing a specific garment.

After Measuring

Take your underbust measurement to the Universal Bra Size Calculator along with your bust measurement. If a calculated size later feels wrong, use the fit checklist rather than automatically adding or removing four inches from the band.

Worked example: underbust of 33 inches

A 33-inch snug underbust rounds to 33 inches. Because the rounded value is odd, BraSizePRO moves to the next even number: 34. That is the site's band starting label, not a guarantee that a 34 band will feel right in every garment.

How to use this page

Some sizing approaches add inches to an underbust reading before selecting a band label. BraSizePRO does not add four or five inches. Methods differ, so avoid treating one formula as a universal rule. Use the documented formula as a reproducible starting point, then check the brand chart and the actual garment.
