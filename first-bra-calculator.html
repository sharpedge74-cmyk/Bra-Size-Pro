Direct answer

This calculator uses underbust and bust measurements to suggest a starting label for a first bra. It does not assume a particular age or stage of development. Some starter garments use band-and-cup labels, while others use general sizes. Comfort, privacy, personal choice, and the garment's own size chart matter most.

How this beginner starting point is calculated

The calculator uses the same simple measurement relationship as the main calculator to produce a starting label. It does not assume a particular age, development stage, or bra construction.

  1. Band: underbust is mapped to the nearest even band in the reference range.
  2. Cup: bust minus underbust determines the US cup index.
  3. Style: the result includes a comfort-oriented starter suggestion, but the garment's own size chart and construction remain the deciding references.

For younger wearers, comfort, privacy, personal preference, and an appropriate garment design matter alongside the numerical label. Measurements can also change over time.

Worked example

Suppose underbust is 28 inches and bust is 29.5 inches. The difference is 29.5 − 28 = 1.5 inches. The calculator uses its underbust rounding rule for the band starting label and maps the difference through its configured cup sequence. This is a starting label only; a camisole or bralette may use a different chart.
