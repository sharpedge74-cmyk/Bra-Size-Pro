Measure the Starting Size

Take the measurements requested by the calculator, then use the result as a starting point. Sports-bra labels can be bra-sized or alpha-sized, and the manufacturer's chart should take precedence for the actual garment.

Evaluate the Fit

  • Underband: should remain secure and level during movement.
  • Cups: should contain the breast without persistent bulging or excessive empty space.
  • Underwire, if present: should follow the breast crease rather than sit on breast tissue.
  • Straps: should stay secure without needing to carry the primary load.

These checks are consistent with the University of Portsmouth's published best-fit criteria. (White & Scurr 2012, Ergonomics)

What Research Says About Sports-Bra Performance

A 2020 study tested 98 sports bras and found breast-movement reduction ranging from 36% to 74%. The researchers identified encapsulation style, padded cups, nylon, an adjustable underband and high neck drop as characteristics that together explained 37.1% of the variance in performance.

A separate study found breast-movement asymmetry was common among participants and that sports bras changed the prevalence and magnitude of that asymmetry. The findings support allowing individual adjustment of the underband and straps rather than assuming one universal construction fits everyone.

Support Is About the Garment, Not Just the Cup Letter

Sports-bra performance depends on construction, fit and activity. The evidence does not support a simple rule that assigns compression to one cup range and encapsulation to another.

Start With Your Everyday Measurements

There is usually no need to invent a completely different body-measurement system just because a bra is designed for exercise. Start with reliable underbust and bust measurements, then check whether the sports-bra brand sells by bra size, alpha size such as S/M/L, or a separate activity-specific chart.

If the brand provides its own measurement chart, use it for the final selection. Sports bras vary widely in construction, stretch and intended level of support.

Think About the Activity

A comfortable walking bra and a bra intended for running do not necessarily need to feel or behave the same way. Running and other high-impact activities create repeated breast movement, so stability becomes especially important.

Research on sports-bra design has examined features such as encapsulation, compression, underband construction and neckline. The practical lesson is not that one design always wins; it is that support comes from the combination of garment construction and fit.

A Quick Movement Check

Once the bra is on, move the way you intend to use it. Raise your arms, bend forward, twist your torso and, where appropriate, do a few controlled movements that resemble your activity.

  • The band should remain secure rather than repeatedly shifting.
  • The cups should contain tissue without persistent spillover.
  • Straps should remain in place without excessive pressure.
  • The bra should allow the movement you need without feeling unstable.

Do Not Automatically Size Down

A common temptation is to choose a smaller sports bra because it “feels more supportive.” Too much compression can simply create discomfort without solving a poor fit. Start with the manufacturer's chart and adjust based on how the actual garment behaves.
