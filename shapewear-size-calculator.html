Direct answer

This calculator compares your waist and hip measurements with the site's stored reference ranges to suggest a starting size. Its compression selector changes the guidance, not the size calculation. Because shapewear varies by cut, fabric, compression design, and brand, use the result as a reference and check the chart for the exact garment.

How the shapewear starting size is calculated

The size reference uses waist and hip measurements against the site's shapewear sizing data. The selected compression level changes the guidance shown after the calculation; it does not justify automatically choosing a smaller size.

  1. Measure: enter your natural waist and fullest hip measurements.
  2. Size match: the calculator compares both measurements with the available size ranges.
  3. Compression: light, medium, or firm is treated as a garment-use preference and guidance input rather than a mathematical size-down rule.

Shapewear construction, fabric stretch, garment length, and manufacturer sizing vary. Check the specific product chart and its stated compression guidance.

Worked example

Suppose your waist measures 29 inches and your hips measure 39 inches. The stored reference table lists M for a 28–29-inch waist and 38–39-inch hips, so both measurements fall in that row. The result is M in this site's reference table—not a guarantee that every medium shapewear garment will fit or provide a particular level of compression.

The calculator's light, medium, and firm options do not automatically move the result down or up a size. Follow the product's own size and compression guidance.
