Direct answer

BraSizePRO calculates a starting label from snug underbust and standing bust measurements. It rounds the underbust to a whole inch, moves to the next even band value when needed, and rounds the bust-minus-underbust difference to choose a cup position. The method is reproducible, but it is not a universal standard or a guarantee of garment fit.

What this page documents

This page explains the current formula, regional label mappings, and limits of the main calculator. The calculation is a reference point; the manufacturer's chart and the fit of the actual bra remain important.

ISO 8559-1 defines clothing-related anthropometric measurement terms, and ISO 8559-3 describes a methodology for constructing body-measurement tables and intervals. Neither standard provides a single universal bra-size formula. ISO 8559-1 · ISO 8559-3

Step 1: Calculate the band starting point

The calculator takes the snug underbust measurement in inches.

Band input → nearest whole inch → next even number when needed.

For example, a snug underbust of 32.2 inches becomes 32. A measurement of 31.2 inches rounds to 31 and then moves to the next even value, 32.

The current accepted calculation range is 27 through 51 inches. The displayed band therefore runs from 28 through 50. This is a boundary of Bra Size's calculation model, not a claim that bodies outside this range cannot be fitted.

Step 2: Calculate the cup index

The cup calculation uses the difference between standing bust and snug underbust:

Cup difference = standing bust − snug underbust

The difference is rounded to the nearest whole inch and used as an index into the selected cup sequence. Negative differences are treated as zero.

Worked example

Suppose the entered measurements are:

  • Snug underbust: 32 inches
  • Standing bust: 36 inches
  • Target system: US

The band calculation keeps 32 as the even band value. The bust difference is 36 − 32 = 4 inches. A rounded difference of 4 maps to D in the US sequence. The calculator therefore displays 32D as the measurement-based starting point.

That does not mean every 32D bra will fit the same way. The actual garment may differ in cup shape, wire geometry, stretch, depth, and grading. Research comparing bra-fitting criteria also demonstrates that different fitting approaches can produce different recommended sizes. White & Scurr

How sister sizes are generated

For the supported cup sequences, the calculator moves the band by 2 and moves one cup position in the opposite direction:

  • Snugger-band sister: band − 2, cup + 1 position.
  • Looser-band sister: band + 2, cup − 1 position.

For example, a US 32D produces 30DD and 34C when both alternatives remain inside the calculator's supported boundaries.

These are adjacent sizing references, not guarantees of identical fit. Cup construction, wire shape, fabric, and manufacturer grading can change how the garment feels and fits.

What the calculator does not claim

  • It does not establish a medically or anatomically correct bra size.
  • It does not claim that one sizing formula works for every manufacturer.
  • It does not use tight underbust or leaning bust to override the primary calculation.
  • It does not determine whether a specific bra's cup shape, wire, straps, or fabric will fit your body.
  • It does not replace the current size chart supplied by the manufacturer of the garment you are considering.

Why Bra Size documents the method

A calculator is more useful when its assumptions are visible. Bra Size therefore treats its result as a reproducible sizing reference: the same inputs and selected system produce the same result, and the calculation can be inspected rather than presented as a black-box recommendation.

This approach is also consistent with the broader reality of clothing sizing. ISO's clothing-sizing methodology recognizes that body shape and proportions differ across populations and that measurement tables are constructed for particular sizing purposes. The fitting literature likewise shows that different fitting criteria can lead to different recommendations. ISO 8559-3 · White & Scurr

Using the result

  1. Measure consistently using the instructions on the calculator.
  2. Use the result as a starting point rather than a universal answer.
  3. Check the current manufacturer's size chart for the garment you want.
  4. Evaluate the actual garment's band, cups, wires, straps, and overall comfort.
  5. If needed, use the Sister Size Calculator or Bra Fit Checker to explore adjacent sizing and fit considerations.

How we calculated this

Band: convert the snug underbust to inches, round to the nearest whole inch, then move to the next even number if the rounded value is odd.

Cup index: subtract snug underbust from standing bust in inches, round the difference to the nearest whole inch, and use the result as an index in the selected stored cup sequence. Negative differences are set to zero. Values beyond the sequence are limited to the last available label.

Unit handling: centimeter inputs are converted to inches before the main calculation. Displayed conversions are reference mappings maintained by the site, not a universal industry conversion standard.

About the +4/+5 band method

Bra-sizing approaches differ. Some traditional methods add inches to an underbust measurement before selecting a band label. BraSizePRO's calculator does not add four or five inches; it follows the formula documented above. This page does not claim that one method guarantees a better fit for every person or every brand. Compare the calculated starting point with the current product chart and the fit of the garment.
