Direct answer

This calculator uses your current underbust and bust measurements to estimate a maternity or nursing bra starting label. Your selected stage changes the fit guidance, not the underlying calculation. Because measurements and comfort can change during pregnancy and postpartum, treat the result as a snapshot, check the exact brand's chart, and reassess the garment as needed.

How this maternity starting point is calculated

The size estimate uses your current underbust and bust measurements with the same measurement-based starting method used by the main calculator. The pregnancy or postpartum stage changes the fit guidance, not the underlying size calculation.

  1. Band: current underbust is converted to the calculator's reference units and mapped to the nearest even band.
  2. Cup: current bust minus underbust is rounded to a whole-inch cup index and mapped to the US cup sequence.
  3. Stage: the selected stage supplies practical guidance about adjustability and remeasurement; it does not add an assumed number of inches or cup sizes.

Pregnancy and postpartum measurements can change over time, so treat the result as a current starting point and check the specific nursing-bra manufacturer's chart and fit.

Worked example: current measurements

Suppose your underbust is 34 inches and your bust is 39 inches. The difference is 39 − 34 = 5 inches. The calculator applies its underbust rounding rule to choose a band starting label, then maps the five-inch difference through its configured US cup sequence. This is a reproducible label estimate, not a prediction of how your body will change or a guarantee of fit.

Use the result with care

  • Use measurements taken recently, not only a pre-pregnancy label.
  • Check the specific nursing bra's current chart and its adjustment range.
  • Assess comfort, cup coverage, band security, and ease of opening or closing the nursing clips.
  • Recheck fit when the garment no longer feels comfortable; there is no universal schedule that fits everyone.
