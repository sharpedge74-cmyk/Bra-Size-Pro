The Common Sister-Size Rule

If a band is too loose but the cup appears suitable, a common fit experiment is to move down one band and up one cup. If the band is too firm while the cup appears suitable, the reverse move can be tried.

  • 36C → 34D
  • 34D → 36C

These relationships are most useful within the same sizing system. Cross-system conversions require a separate size chart.

Why Sister Sizes Are Not Identical

Moving across bands changes more than the label. The band circumference, wire geometry, cup proportions and strap placement can all change. Therefore a sister size should be treated as an adjacent alternative to test, not as a mathematically identical physical cup.

Use Fit Checks With Sister Sizing

University of Portsmouth research recommends checking the underband, cups, underwire, centre front and straps rather than relying solely on a calculated size. (White & Scurr 2012, Ergonomics)

The exact letters depend on the sizing system. When you cross from US/UK to EU or another system, use that system's cup progression rather than applying the table blindly.

When a Sister Size Is Worth Trying

  • Band too loose, cups close: try one band down and one cup up.
  • Band too firm, cups close: try one band up and one cup down.
  • Cups clearly wrong in shape or coverage: do not assume a sister size will solve it.
