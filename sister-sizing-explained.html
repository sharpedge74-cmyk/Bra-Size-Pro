What Sister Sizes Mean

Sister sizes are nearby bra sizes that can have a similar cup capacity even though the band number and cup letter change. The idea can seem odd at first: a 32D and a 34C have different labels, yet they are commonly treated as related sizes within the same sizing system.

The reason is that cup letters are relative to the band. A D cup does not describe one fixed cup size across every band. As the band number changes, the cup dimensions associated with a letter change too. The Bra Cup Size Guide explains this relationship in more detail.

The exact cup letters depend on the sizing system and its letter sequence. This table illustrates the common US/UK-style pattern; it is not a universal conversion chart for every manufacturer.

Why the Cup Letter Changes

A cup letter is read together with its band number. Keeping the same letter while changing the band also changes the cup dimensions. Sister sizing accounts for that relationship by moving the cup label in the opposite direction when the band changes.

That relationship is approximate. A garment's cup shape, wire width, depth, fabric stretch and strap placement can vary between sizes and brands, so related labels do not mean that two bras are built identically.

Reading a Sister-Size Example

Take 34D as an example. A commonly listed smaller-band sister size is 32DD, while a larger-band sister size is 36C. The band number changes by two, and the cup label moves one position in the opposite direction.

This describes how the labels relate on a size chart. It does not mean the band will feel equally firm or that the cups will have precisely the same shape in every style.

Sister Sizes and International Labels

US, UK, EU, Australian and other sizing systems do not use identical cup-letter progressions or band labels in every range. A sister-size relationship should be read within its original system before comparing it with another country's labels.
