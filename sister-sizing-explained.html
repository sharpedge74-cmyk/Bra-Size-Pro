The Mathematics of Sister Sizes

When a lingerie pattern maker grades bra sizes, the cups do not stay the same physical size across different bands. As the band number gets larger, the cup letter holds more volume.

This means:

  • 30D = 32C = 34B = 36A (All hold ~480 cc of breast volume)
  • 30DD = 32D = 34C = 36B = 38A (All hold ~590 cc of breast volume)
  • 30E = 32DD = 34D = 36C = 38B (All hold ~710 cc of breast volume)
  • 30F = 32E = 34DD = 36D = 38C (All hold ~850 cc of breast volume)

When to Sister Size Down (Tighter Band, Larger Cup)

Sister size down if:

  1. The back band is riding up your back instead of resting straight and level.
  2. The straps are digging into your shoulders because the band isn't holding any weight.
  3. You can hook the bra on the tightest clasp on the day you buy it.

Example: If you are wearing a 36C and the band is loose, try a 34D. The cups will feel identical, but the band will securely anchor to your ribs!

When to Sister Size Up (Looser Band, Smaller Cup)

Sister size up if:

  1. The band leaves bruised, painful welts on your ribcage even after testing the cups backward.
  2. You are buying a brand known for very stiff or non-stretch band fabrics (such as Wacoal or Polish brands).
  3. You have sensitive ribs, asthma, costochondritis, or fibromyalgia.
